# Exhibit Stamp

Put an exhibit sticker — "Plaintiff's Exhibit 12" — anywhere on a page. The
number moves on to the next one by itself, so you can work through a stack of
exhibits without typing a number each time.

## Why you'd use it

You're marking up exhibits for a deposition or a hearing and you need each one
labeled. Stamping them one at a time and keeping the count straight in your head
is where mistakes come from. Here the count is kept for you, and you can correct
it whenever you need to.

## How to do it

1. Choose **Exhibit Stamp**, from the toolbar or the **Annotate** tools — the
   **Exhibit stamps** card opens.
2. Click the stamp you want. Each one shows the exact label its next placement
   will use.
3. Click the page where the sticker should go. It lands centered on your click.
4. Keep clicking to stamp more — the number moves on after each one.
5. Press **Esc**, or pick another tool, when you're finished stamping.

## The number

- **It moves on when a stamp lands on a page**, not when you pick the stamp. So
  opening the card and changing your mind never uses up an exhibit number.
- **To set where it resumes**, type into the **Next** box on that stamp's card —
  a number ("12") or a letter ("AB"). **Start over** takes it back to the first
  exhibit.
- **Deleting the stamp you just placed gives its number back.** Deleting an older
  one doesn't, because the numbers after it are already on the page.
- **To fix one sticker on its own**, right-click it and choose **Edit label...**
  (or double-click it). That changes only that sticker — the running count and
  every other stamp stay where they are.
- **The count is per stamp design and stays on this computer.** Plaintiff's and
  Defendant's exhibits keep separate numbering.

## What to know

- **Move or resize it like anything else.** Drag the sticker to move it; drag a
  corner to resize. The wording, border, and rounding all scale together, so it
  stays the same design at a different size.
- **It stays editable after you reopen the file.** Save, close, come back — the
  stickers are still live objects you can move, renumber, or delete. Use
  **Make markup permanent** when you want them baked into the page for good.
- **On a very large PDF you can add stamps but not change saved ones.** New
  stickers save normally; moving or renumbering one that's already in the file
  isn't available at that size yet.

## Design your own

**New design...** opens the full sticker designer, with a live preview that
updates as you go:

- **Wording** — the name, the text before the number, optional text after it
  ("Exhibit 12 to Deposition"), and how it numbers (1, 2, 3 / A, B, C / no
  number at all), plus whether the wording stacks on two lines or sits inline
  with the number.
- **Size** — three starting presets (Classic, Compact, Wide), or a custom
  width and height from half an inch to four inches.
- **Font** — Helvetica, Times, or Courier, bold and italic, and the font size
  (it still shrinks to fit the sticker if the label doesn't).
- **Colors** — the ink, the background (or none, for a transparent sticker),
  and the border (or none), plus how thick the border is and how rounded its
  corners are.

Every card in the gallery also has **Edit design...**, so you can restyle a
sticker you've already been using. Editing a design never touches its running
count — the number picks up exactly where it left off — and stamps you've
already placed keep the look they had when you placed them; restyling the
design only changes what the *next* stamp from that card looks like.

**Duplicate** copies a design under a new name with its own count starting
from the first exhibit, useful for a near-identical sticker for a different
party or matter. The up/down arrows on a card reorder the gallery. **Delete**
asks you to confirm first, since a design's history goes with it; deleting
the one you have armed clears the tool until you pick another.
